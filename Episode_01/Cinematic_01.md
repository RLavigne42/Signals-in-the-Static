## **Cinematic Landscape Imagery — “Glitch Palace” (2030, Post-Disconnect)**

A dead boulevard stretches beneath a collapsed overpass, its concrete ribs exposed like broken bone. Wind pushes grit in thin sheets across the asphalt, polishing old lane paint into pale ghosts. The husks of cars sit where they died—doors ajar, frames rust-bloomed, windshields webbed and opaque with dust.

Billboards loom at odd angles, their vinyl skins shredded into long ribbons that snap and whisper. Faded ads for vanished conveniences—streaming, delivery, instant everything—hang like jokes no one laughs at anymore. Tangled cables droop from snapped poles, swaying gently, useless as vines.

Ahead, the street is bruised with color.

A neon marquee flickers against the ruin—letters missing, circuitry tired—spelling **GLITCH PLACE** in a stutter of pink and cyan. The light spills across cracked storefront glass and puddles that never fully dry, turning stagnant water into trembling mirrors. Each pulse of neon makes the surrounding decay look briefly staged, like a set that remembers what it used to be.

The building itself is a blocky silhouette of the late century—flat roofline, geometric trim—now softened by time and invasion. Ivy and opportunistic weeds climb the walls, threading through broken masonry. A roll-up service door is half-collapsed, its metal slats bent inward like a clenched jaw. Plywood covers windows in mismatched panels, some painted with sun-bleached shapes that hint at arcade icons: a pixelated star, a crooked joystick, a grin made of squares.

The air carries the sharp bite of ozone after a storm, mixed with old dust and damp concrete. Somewhere inside, a low, steady thump vibrates through the boards—more felt than heard—like a heart refusing to admit it’s mortal.

Beyond the arcade, the city is a jagged horizon of skeletal towers. Glass is gone from most of them, leaving dark grids that catch the neon in brief, broken reflections. Rooftop water tanks sit tilted and empty. Satellite dishes—once pointed at a sky full of certainty—now face nowhere in particular, rusted into resignation.

There are no screens glowing in windows. No scrolling signs. No digital chatter bleeding from pockets and wrists. Only the intermittent buzz of failing neon, the hush of wind through exposed steel, and the quiet insistence of a place that should be dead—still lit, still humming, still calling.
