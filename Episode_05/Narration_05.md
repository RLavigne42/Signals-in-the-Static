### Episode 05: The Whisper Network

This is **Charlotte**, broadcasting to you from the hearth of **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**.

You’re tuned to **“Signals in the Static”**—a radio serial from the **Disconnected Frontier**, where the old world survives in scraps of song, half-remembered slogans, and doors that only open for the right kind of believer.

When we last left our trio in the dim side chamber of the Glitch Palace, the Machine Oracle had swallowed their question and choked on the answer, spitting out a burnt, incomplete map coordinate. The glitter-cheeked attendant demanded their payment for a second try: truth, talent, or treasure.

Alex chose to pay clean. Exactly what he had promised.

He didn’t reach for his gear. He didn’t try to bargain with metal. He simply looked the attendant in the eye, bathed in the Oracle’s sickly green glow, and offered a truth with real weight.

“Before the Disconnect,” Alex said, his voice dropping low enough that the synth thump from the main floor couldn’t steal it, “I cataloged things nobody thought mattered. Commercial jingles. Home-recorded mixtapes. Local news broadcasts. The tiny stuff people laughed at—until the day the world went quiet, and that tiny stuff became the only way to remember a voice.”

He paused, the sound of a cassette-ribbon curtain whispering as someone passed by out in the hall.

“My sister used to label everything in block letters. *SIDE A. SIDE B.* When the grids died, she kept labeling anyway—like naming it could keep it real.” Alex tapped the creased, handwritten J-card he had offered earlier. “I’m still doing it. I’m still trying to keep what’s left from getting erased.”

For a second, the attendant’s glitter looked less like a costume and more like dried tears. Their grip on the broom handle finally loosened.

“One more,” they said softly. “Controlled. Clean. You ask smaller. You don’t make it hungry.”

They fed a fresh strip of paper into the dot-matrix printer, their hands practiced, like a projectionist threading old film. The Oracle’s internal fan whined. The green screen jittered. And Mila felt it again—that faint carrier shiver crawling up her spine, a static that simply shouldn't exist in a dead world. But this time, it stayed at the edges. It didn't break into voices.

Alex leaned in and asked his smaller question, careful as placing a needle on vinyl: *“‘East of the Glass’—what is the Glass?”*

The printer began to chatter. This time, it didn’t burn. It stamped.

*THE GLASS = FUSED INTERCHANGE.*
*SUNDOWN RIDGE: FOLLOW THE OLD RAIL.*
*BEARING MARK: 071 (NE).*

The attendant tore the strip off and folded it twice, treating it like sacred paper. “That’s all,” they warned, handing it over. “Don’t make it cough again.”

Nolan exhaled through his nose—relieved, but nowhere near relaxed. “Zero-seven-one, Northeast,” he murmured. “That’s a line we can walk.”

Mila’s eyes lingered on the Oracle just a beat too long. “And that ‘fused interchange’... a glass desert. Heat-blasted. Makes sense.”

They had their bearing. But out on the main floor, a new sound was rising underneath the arcade music. A low, restless murmur, like a crowd noticing a storm rolling in. The whisper network was already moving. Someone out there was asking—too loudly—who had been allowed inside the Oracle's chamber.

Attention is a heavy currency in the Glitch Palace, and the trio had just spent all of theirs. They needed to move, and they needed to do it before the questions turned into a crowd.

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Nolan** take the lead, using subtle body language and misdirection to slip the trio quietly through the arcade, escaping before the whispers curdle into suspicion?

Will **Mila** ask the attendant for permission to step into a back room, pretending she just needs to sketch the bearing onto sturdier paper, while secretly using the moment to hunt for the source of that unnatural static?

Or will **Alex** hold his ground for one more minute, publicly thanking the attendant and performing a loud, vibrant story for the nearby Nostalgists to frame their visit as harmless devotion rather than a dangerous data grab?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Keep your memories close to your chest. And when the room starts whispering your name... don't be there to answer.
