### Episode 04: The Cost of a Question

This is **Charlotte**, broadcasting from **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**.

You’re tuned to **“Signals in the Static”**—a radio serial from the **Disconnected Frontier**, where the old world survives in scraps of song, half-remembered slogans, and doors that only open for the right kind of believer.

When we left our trio, they stood before the Machine Oracle, a hulking arcade cabinet hidden under a denim tarp. The glitter-cheeked attendant had barred the way with a broom handle, demanding truth, talent, or treasure for a single question.

Alex chose the cleanest weapon he owned: a story. Told like a cassette you’ve rewound a hundred times—creased, warm, and true.

“We’re not here to strip-mine your shrine,” Alex said, his voice dropping soft enough to fit the quiet of the side chamber. “We’re here because the old world left breadcrumbs, and someone—someone like you—kept the oven hot.”

He slid a hand into his coat and brought out a single cassette J-card, blank on one side, handwritten on the other. Just a prop. A metaphor.

“Let me ask one clean question,” Alex offered. “And I’ll pay clean: a truth. A story you can repeat when you want the room to listen.”

The attendant’s broom handle lowered an inch. “Truth costs. But I like your tone. One ask. No digging. No second tries.”

They pulled the tarp back.

The Oracle’s screen flickered to life. Green phosphor and crawling scanlines cut through the dim room. The attendant typed a ritual prompt on the grafted Commodore keyboard, and as the keys clacked, a sharp snap of static tore through the air like Velcro.

Mila felt it instantly—a thin, wrong shiver, like a radio caught between stations. Nolan felt it too, shifting his stance to keep the entrance in his periphery.

Alex sat on the squeaking stool. The cabinet’s fan whined like a dying deck chewing tape. On the green screen, two lines pulsed: *ASK CLEAN. PAY CLEAN.*

Alex asked carefully, “This map. Military ink, pre-Disconnect. Where does it actually point?”

The Oracle answered in its own language. A side-mounted dot-matrix printer screamed to life, letters punching through the paper like tiny gunfire. But halfway through, the printer caught. The screen jittered. Another hard snap of static popped in the chamber, louder this time.

The attendant cursed under their breath. “It does that when people ask hungry questions,” they said, yanking the paper free.

The printout was scorched at the edges, like someone had held it too close to a candle. Mila leaned in, her eyes tracking the charred text.

“Sundown Ridge,” she read. “East of the Glass.” But the third line—the numbers, the actual coordinates—was burned into illegibility.

“Those were coordinates,” she muttered.

Nolan’s head tilted toward the doorway. “And now someone knows we asked.”

Because out on the main floor, the heavy thrum of the arcade had changed. A sudden hush rippled through the neon-lit crowd, followed by a whisper-thread moving like a dropped needle on vinyl: *“Oracle spat paper.”*

Attention is currency in the Glitch Palace, and our trio just spent a lot of it.

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Mila** attempt a forensic recovery, using pencil pressure and a quick mechanical tweak on the dot-matrix printer to coax out an echo of those missing digits?

Will **Alex** pay the promised truth, offering the attendant a compelling story of real loss to earn one more controlled attempt without breaking the ritual?

Or will **Nolan** cut the spotlight, pulling the trio away from the Oracle and melting into the crowd to quietly question regulars about “Sundown Ridge” before the whispers turn into a threat?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Keep your voice steady near machines that remember. And keep one ear on the noise beneath the music.
