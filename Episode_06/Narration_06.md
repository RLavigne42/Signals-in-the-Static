### Episode 06: Observation

This is **Charlotte**, speaking to you from the hearth-warm glow of **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**.

You’re listening to **“Signals in the Static,”** a little story we keep alive the old-fashioned way: breath, firelight, and the courage to say what happened out loud, even when the world would rather you swallowed it.

Last episode ended with a choice made in a room full of neon and whispers. The Glitch Palace had started to notice our trio. The Oracle had given up its bearing, and the crowd had begun to ask the wrong kind of questions.

Alex chose not to run. He stayed one more minute.

He stepped out of that side chamber with the folded dot-matrix strip tucked away like a prayer you don’t show strangers. And then he lifted his voice—not as a performance, not as a plea, but as something steadier. A man speaking like he belonged there.

“Hey,” he said, nodding toward the glitter-cheeked attendant with genuine respect. “Your Oracle’s still breathing. That’s not luck—that’s care.”

Heads turned. A girl in fingerless gloves paused mid-sip from a dented canteen. Someone at a Galaga cabinet leaned back, curious. The room, which had been tightening around them like a fist, loosened just enough to breathe.

Alex kept going, letting the words land like a slow synth line. “People think the eighties were neon and noise. But the real magic was simpler: somebody hit record because they thought a moment mattered. Somebody kept a thing running because they thought a memory mattered.”

He tapped his chest once. “That’s the whole vibe. Not the plastic. Not the pose. The keeping.”

And the attendant—who’d been a gate and a guard a moment ago—shifted into something protective. They raised that broom handle like a staff and called out, half-annoyed, half-proud: “Oracle time’s done. Show’s over. Go feed a cabinet.”

Just like that, the room exhaled. The whisper-thread changed shape. Not *“Who are they?”* but *“Oh. They're devotees.”* Harmless. Belonging.

**Nolan** used the moment to drift a step left, subtle as smoke, putting his body between the trio and the main entrance sightline. **Mila** kept her hands still, but her eyes didn’t stop moving, measuring wiring, corners, and faces the way a person does when they’ve learned danger doesn’t always announce itself.

And then the payoff turned sharp.

At the curtain of cassette ribbons near the entrance, a pair of strangers appeared. Too plain. Too clean. No patches. No glitter. No tokens. Their gaze didn’t wander the way a tourist’s would. It landed like a finger pointing. It targeted.

Nolan caught it first. A micro-nod. A warning passed without words. Not a raid. Not yet. Just Observation—the kind that comes right before fire.

The attendant leaned close to Alex, their voice suddenly thin. “You did a good thing,” they whispered. “Now do a smart thing. Don’t let them follow you out.”

Mila tilted her head, listening the way some folks listen for thunder in their bones. “That static earlier…” she murmured. “It’s not just the Oracle. Something in this district is bleeding carrier tone.”

Outside those walls, the world is dust and silence. Inside, it’s neon and noise. And somewhere between the two is a line you can trace—if you’re brave or foolish enough.

So now the trio stands in the cabinet rows, with watchers at the edge of the room and a wrong kind of music hiding under the right kind of sound.

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Nolan** melt into the arcade’s moving bodies and shadow those observers from inside, getting close enough to overhear who they answer to before peeling away?

Will **Mila** turn the Glitch Palace’s own wiring into a crude antenna, following that faint carrier shiver to its strongest point and triangulating the source without stepping outside yet?

Or will **Alex** walk straight up with a smile, welcoming them with easy talk and borrowed warmth, forcing them to improvise a cover story while he reads every tell they can’t help but show?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Here’s my advice, for whatever it’s worth: when you feel eyes on your back, don’t sprint. Don’t freeze. Move like you’ve got nothing to hide—and everything to lose.
