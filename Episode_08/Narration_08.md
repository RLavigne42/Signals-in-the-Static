### Episode 08: The Safest Disguise

This is **Charlotte**, broadcasting to you from the hearth-warm glow of **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**. Out here in 2030, after the Disconnect taught us what silence costs, we learn to listen for what people don’t say, and to fear the stories that spread faster than footsteps.

When we last left the Glitch Palace, our trio was being hunted by plainclothes watchers. They needed a way out without triggering a panic or a fight.

Alex chose not to run or hide. Instead, he did what he’s always done best when a room turns dangerous: he weaponized warmth. He didn’t reach for a weapon. He reached for a roomful of witnesses.

He drifted back into a loud, warm pocket of Nostalgists gathered near a battered boombox they’d made into an altar. Denim and neon, cassette tape braided into scarves, laughter thick enough to hide a knife. Alex lifted his hands, palms open, and made himself the center of something harmless.

He told them, bright and easy, that if anyone heard he’d been back there trying to pull secrets from the Oracle, it wasn’t true. He said he’d asked it something small, something almost tender. And then he gave them a line that fit their world like an old chorus.

*“Follow the old rail.”*

It landed. You could feel it take root in the way a phrase becomes a chant. A couple of them laughed. Someone clapped. Someone repeated it like it had always been theirs.

Then Alex leaned in closer, his voice dropping into the tone of a trade. He said he was here for music. For old tapes and handwritten J-cards. For anything with a story pressed into plastic. He offered to trade for a mixtape with a real history behind it.

And just like that, the narrative snapped into place around him. An archivist. A tape-hunter. A harmless pilgrim of nostalgia. Not a map-chaser. Not a bunker-seeker. Not someone worth dragging out into the open alleyway.

Across the room, **Nolan** didn’t relax. He watched the main entrance through the dark glass of the arcade cabinets, using the reflections the way a careful man uses cover. The Analog Order scouts were still somewhere in the crowd, their faces lost among strangers, but their attention had dulled. In a place like the Glitch Palace, where people came to pretend the old world still had rules, the scouts couldn’t make a move without becoming the problem everyone would remember.

**Mila**, though, kept hearing it. That thin, needle-bright carrier tone humming under the arcade’s noise, like a radio trying to clear its throat. It wasn’t loud, but it was persistent, and it grew stronger near the back wall where thick conduit ran into the building’s bones.

Then, a door marked *STAFF ONLY* rattled once, bumped from the other side. A breath of fryer oil and wet concrete slipped through the crack, and with it, the promise of a way out that wasn’t the main door. An exit route. A risk. A chance.

The air in the Glitch Palace felt watchful now. Not panicked, but alert in that quiet way that comes before trouble decides what shape it wants to take. Alex’s story was spreading, buying them a narrow window where no one could be sure what they’d seen.

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Nolan** take the staff-only door and guide the trio into the service corridors, moving fast and quiet, keeping them away from the scouts’ likely line of sight?

Will **Mila** press to the back wall and trace that carrier tone through the conduit, using the arcade’s wiring like an antenna to find the source inside the building before they leave?

Or will **Alex** seal the decoy by trading for a cheap, harmless mixtape right there, making his cover story true enough to stand on before slipping out with the crowd while the chant still has teeth?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Remember: in a crowded room, the safest disguise isn’t a mask. It’s a story everyone wants to repeat.
