## 1) SCENE TITLE (1 line)
**GLITCH PALACE: The Oracle Under Neon**

## 2) ESTABLISHING SHOT (environment as a “camera pan”)
The Glitch Palace squats in the ribs of an old strip-mall husk, where the parking lot has become a shallow marsh of oil-slick rainwater and knee-high weeds. A dead marquee hangs above the entrance like a broken jaw—letters missing, wiring exposed, the whole sign stitched together with copper twists and hope. Every few breaths, a pulse of neon coughs to life behind cracked plastic tubing, painting the puddles pink, then dying back to black.

You approach through a corridor of abandoned storefronts: shattered glass, sun-bleached posters, and cables that droop from the ceiling like vines that never learned how to grow. The air smells of wet concrete and rust, with a faint bite of ozone—like someone’s been coaxing power out of a generator that doesn’t want to be awake.

From inside, sound leaks through the seams: coin-chimes, synth stabs, the thump of a bass line that’s too steady to be natural. It’s not music so much as a heartbeat someone’s forcing into a corpse.

## 3) THRESHOLD MOMENT (crossing into the interior)
The door gives with a sticky reluctance, and the world changes temperature. Outside is damp and honest; inside is warm in patches, like heat trapped in old carpet and overworked transformers. The light turns sickly and theatrical—neon bleeding across cracked tile, CRT glow washing faces in green-blue like moonlight filtered through aquarium glass.

Dust lies everywhere except where it doesn’t. There are clean arcs on the floor where feet have worn paths between cabinets. A smear of fresh grime on a doorframe. A faint warmth radiating from a machine that should’ve been dead for years. The Glitch Palace doesn’t welcome you. It tests you.

## 4) KEY DETAILS (bullet list, 6–12 items)
- A row of arcade cabinets with **hand-painted halos** and taped-on “blessings” (opportunity: someone here knows how to keep old boards alive).
- **Dance pads worn down to foam**, the metal edges exposed like teeth (danger: trip hazard; also a perfect place to hide a wire or blade).
- A Pac-Man cabinet with a **coin slot sealed by wax** and a token bowl beside it (ritual: pay the machine “clean”).
- A wall of **dead payphones** converted into message boxes—paper notes, string, and stamped wax seals (opportunity: local intel network).
- A ceiling fan that turns only when the neon surges, **stuttering like it’s afraid** (danger: power is unstable; surges can fry relics).
- Graffiti in thick marker: **“NO DIGITAL. NO GHOSTS.”** crossed out and rewritten: **“ONLY GHOSTS.”**
- A side door with a **freshly replaced padlock** and a new chain (danger: someone is actively controlling access).
- A faint **burnt-plastic smell** near the back wall (danger: overheating transformer or short).
- A denim-and-bandana tarp stitched like a quilt over something large (opportunity: hidden relic).
- A broom handle laid across a stool like a **gate bar** (danger: social barrier; you don’t pass without permission).

## 5) PEOPLE IN THE ROOM (who’s here and how they move)
Nostalgists drift between cabinets like worshippers, jackets patched with band logos and arcade tokens sewn on like medals. They touch machines with two fingers first—like testing holy water—then commit their whole palm once the cabinet “accepts” them. Some mouth old slogans they barely understand, the way folks recite prayers they learned as children.

But there are watchers mixed in with the faithful: scav-lean bodies, eyes that don’t linger on the screens but on hands, pockets, exits. The crowd parts and closes like a curtain—friendly until it isn’t. Here, reverence is a costume anyone can wear, and the ones who wear it best are often the ones with knives.

## 7) THE RELIC / MACHINE / FOCUS OBJECT (make it tactile)
The side chamber sits at the far edge of the noise, where the bass dulls and the air feels older—stale in a way that suggests sealed rooms and long-held breath. Under a tarp stitched from denim and bandanas, something hulks with the patient weight of a thing that has outlasted its makers.

When the cloth shifts, you catch glimpses: a cabinet face scarred by old impacts, corners reinforced with scavenged metal brackets, screws mismatched like teeth from different mouths. And grafted into the front—wrong and perfect at the same time—a **Commodore 64 keyboard**, keys yellowed, letters half-worn away, the plastic polished by hands that came here to bargain with fate.

Above it, spray paint drips in thick strokes, as if the wall itself is sweating the words:

**THE MACHINE ORACLE**  
**ASK CLEAN. PAY CLEAN. LEAVE CLEAN.**

That’s the rule here. Clean coin. Clean hands. Clean question. No spitting, no blood, no lies you can’t afford to have repeated. Folks say the Oracle remembers what you feed it—and that it charges interest.

## 8) THE UNDER-SIGNAL (signature tension device)
At first it’s nothing. Just the usual grit in the air, the usual hiss that lives in old speakers and tired wires. Then it sharpens—thin as a whisper, keen as a blade drawn slow.

It isn’t coming from the cabinets. It isn’t riding the music. It’s deeper in the building, behind walls that were never meant to be opened again. The bass line keeps thumping, steady and loud, like someone’s trying to drown out a confession.

You see it in the attendant’s face: a fractional change, a tightening around the eyes. They heard it too. And the room—without anyone saying a word—leans a little closer to the side chamber, like a crowd sensing a storm before the first drop hits.

## 9) CHARLOTTE RADIO FRAME (required)
This is Charlotte, broadcasting from the Red Griffin Inn in Oakhaven…

In the years since the Disconnect, we learned a hard truth: when the lights go scarce, people start calling any glow a miracle. And when a machine survives long enough, folks stop asking *how* it works and start asking *what it wants*. The Glitch Palace is full of believers—but belief is just another kind of currency, and it spends fast when the static turns sharp.

Keep your voice steady near machines that remember. And keep one ear on the noise beneath the music.

## 10) PLAYER HOOK + CHOICES (interactive ending)
The tarp stirs—not from a draft, but from within. A sickly green glow leaks at the seams like an old monitor waking in a room that swore it was dead. The Commodore keys click once by themselves—one lonely test-press—like something unseen just tried to spell your name.

Mila freezes mid-reach.  
Alex’s hand tightens on the map through his coat.  
Nolan’s eyes cut to the doorway—because the static is getting louder, and the bass is starting to sound like it’s covering for it.

So tell me—what happens next?

**A) Offer a clean story and a single clean question.**  
- What you do: Alex steps forward, palms open, and trades a careful truth for one question—no extra payment, no theatrics.  
- What it risks: the Oracle (or its handler) may take “clean” to mean *confession*, and you can’t un-say a thing in a room that hoards secrets.  
- What it might reveal or gain: the Oracle’s rules, its limits, and whether the static is a warning… or an invitation.

**B) Lift the tarp and inspect the wiring—hands-on stabilization.**  
- What you do: Mila breaks the shrine’s boundary, peels back cloth, and puts her fingers where only the attendant’s are supposed to go.  
- What it risks: social backlash, a sudden power surge, or waking the machine wrong—some relics bite when startled.  
- What it might reveal or gain: the Oracle’s true power source, any hidden transmit/receive hardware, and a chance to tame the under-signal before it escalates.

**C) Work the room—barter for tokens, favors, and the real chain of control.**  
- What you do: Nolan melts into the crowd, trading small goods and smaller truths to learn who controls Oracle access and why the padlock is new.  
- What it risks: getting marked as a thief, drawing attention from watchers, or losing time while the static sharpens into something worse.  
- What it might reveal or gain: Oracle tokens, a back-channel to the handler, and the name of whoever’s listening from deeper in the building.
