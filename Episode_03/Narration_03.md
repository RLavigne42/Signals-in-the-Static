### Episode 03: The Machine Oracle

This is **Charlotte**, broadcasting from **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**.

You’re tuned to **“Signals in the Static”**—a radio serial from the **Disconnected Frontier**, where the old world survives in scraps of song, half-remembered slogans, and doors that only open for the right kind of believer.

When we left our trio, they were standing on the neon threshold of the Glitch Palace, the bouncer demanding a password to prove they belonged.

Alex went diplomatic—half confession, half performance. He lifted both hands, palms open in the neon spill.

“That line’s from *Back to the Future*,” he said, his voice easy, like he was letting them in on a secret instead of begging for a door. “And the car… c’mon, you know this. Steel gullwings, nuclear dream. The DeLorean.”

The shorter bouncer’s mirrored lenses caught the sign’s cyan flicker. He nodded once—slow, approving. The taller one leaned forward, his cassette still spinning on his finger like a tiny planet.

“Say it like you mean it,” he growled, but the edge was gone. It was ritual now, not a threat.

Alex didn’t flinch. “DeLorean. Time machine. Bad ideas. Great soundtrack.”

A beat. Then the taller bouncer laughed—short and sharp—and slapped the door twice in a coded rhythm. The bolts clacked. The Glitch Palace exhaled warm air that smelled like hot dust, old carpet, and burnt sugar.

And out on the street behind them, that silhouette on the rooftop shifted just once, taking note, before vanishing into the broken skyline. They were in.

Inside, the world went electric in every way that still worked. Flickering CRT glow, neon tubes, and a restless chorus of coin-chimes and 8-bit arpeggios echoing off cracked tile. Nostalgists drifted between the cabinets like worshippers, their jackets patched with band logos and arcade tokens sewn on like medals.

The bouncers waved them through a curtain of dangling cassette tape ribbons. They moved past Dance Dance Revolution pads worn down to the foam, past a Pac-Man cabinet with a hand-painted halo, until they found it in a side chamber.

A hulking cabinet sitting under a tarp of stitched denim and bandanas. Someone had wired a Commodore 64 keyboard into its face, like a priest grafting scripture into an idol. Above it, a sign in dripping spray paint read:

*THE MACHINE ORACLE. ASK CLEAN. PAY CLEAN. LEAVE CLEAN.*

A Nostalgist attendant—cheeks smeared with glitter, hair teased into an impossible silhouette—blocked the stool with a broom handle.

“Oracle time ain’t free,” they said, their gaze flicking over the trio. “What’re you feeding it: truth, talent… or treasure?”

Mila’s fingers twitched, already reading the wiring by instinct, itching to touch it. Alex felt the map hidden in his coat like a pulse. And Nolan… Nolan heard the bass thump of the arcade, but right under it, he heard the faintest suggestion of static. Not coming from the speakers. Coming from somewhere deeper.

And now, the Oracle waits for its tribute.

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Alex** step up and offer a careful story, using diplomacy and old-world lore to convince the attendant they are worthy of a single, clean ask without paying extra?

Will **Mila** bypass the attendant and inspect the Oracle’s wiring herself, using her engineering skills to stabilize the machine and earn access, even if it means touching a system they treat like a sacred relic?

Or will **Nolan** turn his back on the machine and work the room, quietly bartering with nearby Nostalgists for Oracle tokens, keeping their own relic hidden while he figures out who really controls the power in this room?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Keep your voice steady near machines that remember. And keep one ear on the noise beneath the music.
