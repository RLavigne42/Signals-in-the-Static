### Episode 09: The Decoy Tape

This is **Charlotte**, coming to you from **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**, and you’re listening to **“Signals in the Static.”** In a world where the grid went dark and never came back, we learned to measure danger the old way: by the weight of eyes on your back, and the way a room’s noise can turn into cover.

When we last left the Glitch Palace, our trio was looking for an exit. Alex knew the best way out was to make his decoy real.

He slipped into the Nostalgist circle like he belonged to the glow, like the neon had always known his name. His voice stayed gentle, eager in the safe way, hungry for stories instead of secrets. He asked to see what they had, and a kid with one side of their head shaved clean produced a cassette from an inside pocket as if it were a blade.

The paper insert was hand-lettered in thick marker, the kind of ink that doesn’t apologize for existing. *FRIDAY NIGHT DRIVE // 1987 (RECORDING). DO NOT DUB.*

“Found it in a glovebox,” the kid said. “A little warped, but it plays. You trade?”

Alex didn’t blink. He offered something harmless but convincing: a clean blank cassette in an intact case, and a strip of dot-matrix paper pulled from his satchel—old, official-looking, and useless to anyone hunting bunkers. The kid’s eyes widened at the paper alone. The deal snapped shut like a latch.

The circle cheered as if they’d just watched a rite completed properly. Someone slapped Alex’s shoulder. The rumor hardened into fact, the way wet cement becomes a wall: the archivist came for tapes, the archivist traded, the archivist was safe in the only way a stranger can be safe in a place like this.

Across the arcade, the air still carried that needle-bright carrier tone, thin as fishing line, tugging at **Mila’s** attention. It threaded itself through the muffled bass from the boombox altar, through the clatter of old cabinets and the soft shuffle of bodies. **Nolan** kept scanning reflections in dark glass, hunting for the too-clean faces that didn’t match the room.

Near the front, just for a blink, there was one of them: a shoulder, a profile, a watcher trying to find the thread they’d lost. But the crowd owned Alex now. Any hand reaching for him would have to cut through witnesses, and witnesses in a Nostalgist sanctuary had long memories.

The glitter-cheeked attendant's look slid across the trio like a match struck in the dark. *Now,* it said. Not later. Not after you think. *Now.*

The boombox altar thumped out a muffled bassline, and dust danced in the neon like glitter that forgot it was supposed to settle. Somewhere deeper in the building, the *STAFF ONLY* door breathed again, exhaling fryer-oil air and damp concrete, an alleyway promise dressed up as a workplace necessity.

Mila barely moved her lips when she spoke, as if even her breath might be overheard. “That carrier’s stronger when the sign flickers,” she murmured. “Like it’s riding the building’s metal.”

Nolan answered without turning his head. “We don’t solve mysteries while we’re being watched.”

Alex held the newly traded tape close, not hiding it, not flaunting it, just letting it be seen like a passport. “We can do both,” he said, “if we do it like we belong.”

So tell me, traveler—what happens next?

Will **Nolan** take point through the *STAFF ONLY* door and guide them into the service corridors, fast and quiet, keeping them off the main exit’s sightline?

Will **Mila** move along the back wall with her palm brushing conduit and metal frames, tracking where that carrier tone peaks until she can lock the northeast bearing before they slip away?

Or will **Alex** ask the boombox circle for a "listening corner" to test the traded tape, using playback as a natural reason to drift toward the back rooms and the exits?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Remember what the old-timers used to say about crowded rooms: the safest path isn’t always the fastest one, but it’s the one that looks like it was meant for you.
