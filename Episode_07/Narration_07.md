### Episode 07: The Wrong Question

This is **Charlotte**, coming to you from the hearth-warm glow of **The Red Griffin Inn** in **Oakhaven**, where the fire still knows how to speak and the walls still remember what it means to keep a secret.

You’re tuned to **“Signals in the Static,”** a radio serial from the **Disconnected Frontier**, where the brightest lights can’t always hide the darkest intentions, and where a single wrong glance can follow you for miles.

When we last left the Glitch Palace, our trio stood with watchers at the edge of the room and a thin, needle-bright wrongness humming under the arcade’s noise. They had to decide how to handle the plainclothes strangers who were observing them entirely too closely.

Alex chose to walk straight at them with a smile.

Not a challenge. Not a threat. A social trap. The kind that turns quiet surveillance into a problem you have to talk your way out of.

He threaded through the crowd like he belonged there, the neon flicker striping his face in cold cyan and magenta. The two strangers by the cassette-ribbon curtain didn’t move until he was close enough that backing away would look like guilt.

Alex greeted them like it was some old-world food court, like strangers were just friends he hadn’t met yet. He called out how underpatched they looked for the Glitch Palace, asked if it was their first time, and offered a cabinet recommendation or a safe exit with the same easy warmth.

One of them, tall and wind-chapped with hands too clean for this place, simply said they were just passing through.

Alex nodded like that was charming, and kept talking. He forced them to perform normal. He gave them his name, called himself an archivist, asked what their vibe was, asked what brought them in.

They said they’d heard there was a working machine.

And that, right there, was the tell.

The people who come here for comfort don’t call it a "working machine." They call it the Oracle, like it’s a saint behind a curtain. Or they don’t say it at all.

Alex’s smile didn’t change, but the air around it did. He told them Oracle time was done, and if they were here for quiet tech, they were in the wrong neighborhood. The tall one’s mouth twitched—annoyance held on a short leash. His companion’s hand brushed the inside of his coat, not drawing anything, just checking that something was still there.

And then Alex heard it too.

Not the arcade music. Not the crowd. Something thinner, sharper, alive in a way it had no right to be. A carrier tone, riding under everything like a needle riding a groove.

The tall watcher saw Alex notice. His eyes sharpened. He tried to end the conversation with a polite goodbye and step past.

Alex shifted—still courteous, still smiling—but he blocked the path like a door that doesn’t slam, but simply refuses to open. He asked one more question, soft as a handshake and twice as dangerous.

“Who told you about the Oracle?”

The silence that followed had real weight. The kind that settles on your shoulders and makes you aware of every breath you take.

The companion answered instead, his voice flat as dead tape: “Wrong question.”

And then they were gone—slipping sideways into the crowd with practiced ease, vanishing between cabinets and bodies before Nolan could cut them off without turning the whole room into a scene.

No names. No open badge. No confession.

But as they passed beneath a hanging neon sign, Mila caught a glint at the tall one’s belt. An etched metal tag, plain and stamped. A circle with a slash.

**Analog Order.**

They had been close enough that the trio could smell the smoke on their clothes.

After that, the side chamber didn’t feel like shelter anymore. It felt like the walls had heard everything and decided to remember it. The attendant’s broom handle tapped once on the floor—a warning, not applause—and their whisper came sharp and urgent.

“Out,” they hissed. “Not the main door. Not tonight.”

Mila closed her eyes for half a second and turned her head like a human antenna. That carrier tone was stronger near the back wall, where old conduit ran into the building’s bones like fossilized veins. Nolan’s gaze was already measuring the room the way a survivor measures a storm, hunting for service corridors and staff routes—the kind of exits locals use when they don’t want to be seen leaving.

Alex had just forced the watchers to look at his face long enough to remember it.

So tell me, traveler. With Analog Order scouts in the crowd and a living signal bleeding through dead air, what happens next?

Will **Nolan** take point through a staff-only corridor, getting the trio out the back the way locals do, before the Order's attention hardens into something worse?

Will **Mila** follow the conduit deeper into the building, using the arcade’s wiring like an antenna to triangulate the carrier source right now, before they leave and lose the thread?

Or will **Alex** plant a rumor with a nearby Nostalgist circle, turning their Oracle visit into harmless "vibe devotion," muddying the story just enough to slip out during the distraction?

This is **Charlotte**, signing off from **The Red Griffin Inn**. Here’s the warning I’ll leave you with tonight: when the wrong people learn your face, don’t just run. Change the story they think they’re chasing.
